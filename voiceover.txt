How do you build a 726-foot dam inside a flowing river?
In the 1930s, engineers faced this challenge at Black Canyon.
Before building anything, they had to move the Colorado River.
Crews blasted four huge tunnels through the canyon walls.
Combined, those tunnels stretched almost three miles around the worksite.
Next, temporary cofferdams blocked the river and kept the foundation dry.
Excavators dug through sediment until they reached solid bedrock.
Above them, high scalers hung from ropes, clearing unstable canyon walls.
In 1933, crews began pouring concrete, not as one giant slab,
but in hundreds of interlocking blocks, rising layer by layer.
Engineers circulated chilled water through embedded pipes to prevent overheating.
The dam alone required 3.25 million cubic yards of concrete.
The concrete was finished in 1935; the entire project in 1936.
And the result was Hoover Dam, a 726-foot giant built by moving a river.
